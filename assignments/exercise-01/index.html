Exercise 01

GNSS Satellite Orbit Computation, Kepler Equation, ECI/ECEF Transformation, and Orbital Visualization

Assignment 01 Multi-GNSS Orbit Mechanics Numerical Methods

01 — Overview

In this exercise, students investigate GNSS satellite motion through orbital computation, numerical solution of Kepler's equation, coordinate transformations, and visualization of satellite trajectories.

The objective is to connect theoretical orbital mechanics with practical satellite positioning computations.

02 — Objectives

  • Understand and solve Kepler's equation numerically.
  • Compare Newton, Fixed-Point, and Bessel-series approaches.
  • Compute satellite positions in ECEF and ECI frames.
  • Investigate satellite velocity and rotating-frame relations.
  • Evaluate orbital radius and orbital period.
  • Visualize satellite trajectories and ground tracks.

03 — Tasks

Task A — Kepler Equation

Download one day of a multi-GNSS navigation file from IGS and implement the following methods to solve Kepler's equation:

  • Newton's Method
  • Fixed-Point Iteration
  • Bessel Series Expansion

Compare convergence, number of iterations, and accuracy for different eccentricities.

Task B — Satellite Position

Compute satellite ECEF and ECI positions over a 24-hour period using a 30-second sampling interval.

Task C — Velocity and Orbital Analysis

  • Compute satellite velocities.
  • Investigate the relation between rotating and inertial frames.
  • Check orbital radius and orbital period.

Task D — Visualization

  • Generate a 24-hour 3D satellite animation in ECI.
  • Generate a 24-hour 3D satellite animation in ECEF.
  • Plot satellite ground tracks.
  • Optionally use Plotly for interactive visualization.

04 — Expected Deliverables

  • Source code with clearly documented computational steps.
  • Tables comparing numerical methods and convergence.
  • Satellite position and velocity results.
  • Orbital analysis and verification results.
  • ECI/ECEF visualizations and ground tracks.
  • A technical report explaining the results.

05 — Submission

Submission instructions will be announced here.

Please organize your source code, report, tables, and figures in a clear and reproducible structure.

06 — Academic Focus

This assignment focuses on numerical computation, orbital mechanics, coordinate reference systems, and scientific visualization in GNSS.
